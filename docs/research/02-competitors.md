**Main finding:** this is a crowded market. QR or card attendance plus an SMS to parents is already a standard feature, not a unique one.

## What this tells us

### 1. Our main selling point is already taken

"Parents automatically get a 'your child arrived' SMS" is offered by at least tuitionfee.lk, eduX, WST, IT Signature, TutorFee and others. A teacher who sees our product will have seen this before. **It is a required feature, not a reason to switch.**

### 2. Price expectations are set low

For a teacher with about 200 students:

| Product | Cost per month |
|---|---|
| ClassFlow | about Rs. 8,300 a year ≈ **Rs. 700/month** |
| tuitionfee.lk | **Rs. 1,999/month** (plus SMS) |
| eduX | Rs. 15,000/month (the 500-student plan) |

So a new product aimed at single teachers can probably charge about **Rs. 1,000–2,500 a month for 200 students**, and needs a free or very cheap entry plan.

### 3. The leaders compete on different things

- **IT Signature** wins institutes with hardware, a sales team and trust (video testimonials).
- **tuitionfee.lk and ClassFlow** win small teachers on price and simplicity.
- **eduX** wins teachers who want an LMS: online classes, exams and recordings.

### 4. Gaps we could not see anyone fully covering

These are based on public websites only and need confirming in interviews:

- **"Your child did NOT arrive" alerts.** Most products send arrival messages. Few highlight absence alerts, which is what worried parents actually care about.
- **LankaQR fee payment** from the parent's phone, with the receipt sent automatically and the class card updated. Most products record cash or bank-slip uploads.
- **Sinhala and Tamil-first interface.** Most competitor sites are English-first.
- **Offline scanning** that keeps working when the hall's Wi-Fi or mobile data drops, then syncs later.
- **Institute–teacher money split**, where each teacher sees their share and the institute sees its commission, with no spreadsheet.
- **Open, honest pricing**, including SMS cost. Several big players hide their prices.

## How a teacher decides (our hypothesis, to test)

1. Hears about it from **another teacher** or sees it at a busy institute. Word of mouth is everything.
2. Asks: *"Will this save my assistant time at the door?"* and *"Will parents trust it?"*
3. Checks the price against what they pay now, which is often nothing.
4. Tries it on **one class** before moving all classes.

So the product must be **easy to try on one class**, with no hardware purchase and no sales call.
